The one idea

Everything in CTEV hangs off the talus. The navicular, calcaneus and forefoot are all swung inward around its head, so treatment swings them back out around the same head.

Bone and soft tissue findings

  • Talus has a short neck deviated medially and plantar-wards, so its head points in and down. It is the primary abnormality.
  • Navicular is displaced medially and articulates close to the medial malleolus. Cuboid is displaced medially on the calcaneus.
  • Calcaneus is in equinus and varus and rotated under the talus.
  • Soft tissue: posteromedial contracture, a small calf (hypoplastic muscles) and a shorter foot on the affected side.
  • X-ray (rarely needed to start treatment): talus and calcaneus run parallel, so the talocalcaneal angle drops toward zero. The usual normal range is roughly 25 to 50 degrees, and sources differ on exact cutoffs. HY

Severity scores

  • Pirani: 6 clinical signs, each 0, 0.5 or 1, maximum 6. Calculator further down.
  • Dimeglio: four main parameters plus four small ones, maximum 20. Grades: benign below 5, moderate 5 to 10, severe 10 to 15, very severe 15 to 20.

Ponseti, as a set of rules

Start in the first one to two weeks of life while the ligaments are still pliable. Gentle manipulation for one to two minutes, then a long-leg cast with the knee bent to about 90 degrees, changed weekly. Most feet need about five casts. HY

RuleWhy it matters
Fix cavus firstSupinate the forefoot so it lines up with the hindfoot. Until you do, abducting the foot just twists it.
Fulcrum is the talar headThumb on the lateral side of the talar head, abduct the foot under it. The calcaneus then swings out and under the talus on its own.
Never press on the calcaneusIt locks the heel against the talus and stops the abduction you are trying to gain.
Never pronate the forefootPronation deepens cavus. It was the error in the older Kite method, which also used the calcaneocuboid joint as the fulcrum.
Equinus comes lastDorsiflexing before the heel is abducted bends the midfoot instead, which makes a rocker-bottom foot.
Tenotomy if dorsiflexion is under 15°Percutaneous cut about 1 to 1.5 cm above the calcaneal insertion, followed by a final cast for 3 weeks with the foot abducted 60 to 70 degrees.

The brace is the treatment's second half

After the final cast the child wears a foot abduction orthosis, which is straight-last shoes fixed to a Denis Browne bar. Typical schedule is 23 hours a day for 3 months, then nights and naps until about 3 to 4 years. HY

Source books give slightly different angles. The part to remember is that the affected foot is rotated out more than the normal one.

The commonest reason for relapse is that the child stops wearing the brace. A relapse shows up as returning equinus, varus or adductus, usually in the first few years, and is treated with repeat casting.

Score a foot with Pirani

Tap a value for each sign. Three signs describe the hindfoot and three describe the midfoot, and the total runs from 0 (normal) to 6 (worst). HY

0 of 6 · hindfoot 0 · midfoot 0

Scores are tracked cast by cast. Each week the midfoot score should fall as the talar head becomes covered, and tenotomy is considered once the foot is abducted but dorsiflexion is still under 15 degrees.
